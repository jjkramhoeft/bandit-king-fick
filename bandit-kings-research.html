Verdict

  • The README's "2017" is a re-release date. The game first came out in Japan in 1989 on the NEC PC-88. The Western DOS and Mac versions followed in 1989, the NES in 1990 and the Amiga in 1990–91. 2017 is when KOEI Tecmo put the Japanese PC version on Steam.
  • The rules are fully documented and small. The original manuals describe everything: 49 prefectures, 255 characters, 16 monthly commands and a 30-day hex battle. That fits comfortably in a browser game.
  • The code is the easy part. Most of the effort goes into the computer opponents, balancing, and the art: up to 255 portraits, a map, battle tiles, event pictures and around 20 music cues.
  • Build a spiritual successor, not a copy. KOEI Tecmo still sells the game. The Water Margin story and the general mechanics are free to use. The name, art, music, text, exact screen layout and stat tables are not.

01The game, and where "2017" comes from

Bandit Kings of Ancient China is a KOEI historical strategy game based on the Chinese classic Water Margin (水滸伝, Shuihu Zhuan). You play one of the novel's outlaw heroes in early 12th-century Song China. You build up a band of followers and a power base of prefectures, earn the Emperor's trust, and kill the corrupt minister Gao Qiu before northern invaders end the dynasty.36

The 2017 date. VERIFIED On 21 February 2017, KOEI Tecmo released the game on Steam as part of the "Kou Shibusawa Archives", a re-release line for the 35th anniversary of producer Kou Shibusawa. Bandit Kings was in the third round. The Steam build runs only on the Japanese edition of Windows 10.12 No other 2017 release of this game exists, so this is almost certainly where the README's date comes from.

A date discrepancy. The Steam store blurb says the game came out in Japan in 1988. Both Wikipedias, the DOS title screen ("©1989 KOEI") and the release databases say 1989. 1989 is the safer date to use.

02How it plays

Most numbers below come from the US NES manual, cross-checked against the 1991 Amiga manual. The two manuals agree on the core rules. Battle numbers and resource caps were checked only in the NES manual, so the computer versions may differ slightly.

The goal, the clock and the villain VERIFIED

Gao Qiu, the Imperial Minister of War, holds the real power and usually sits in the capital, Kaifeng. You win by defeating his army and killing him. You can attack his outlying prefectures at any time. To attack his home prefecture you need the Imperial Edict. Every January the Emperor looks for a champion: if your Popularity is above 250 at that check, you get the Edict and keep it even if your Popularity later falls.56

You lose if:

  • January 1127 arrives and the Northern Barbarians invade (historically the Jurchen Jin)
  • your Good Fellow dies, is captured, or becomes a Criminal
  • another Good Fellow kills Gao Qiu first

Scenarios and the Good Fellows VERIFIED

There are four scenarios, starting in 1101, 1103, 1104 and 1105. You play one of ten "Good Fellows" from the novel: Lu Zhishen, Shi Jin, Song Jiang, Lin Chong, Wu Song, Yang Zhi, Chao Gai, Li Kui, Li Jun and Li Ying. Scenarios offer 5, 7, 5 and 4 of them. Up to seven people can play hot-seat on one machine, and a zero-player mode lets the computer play itself.5

When you start, you adjust your hero's Body, Strength, Dexterity and Wisdom within that character's limits. Integrity, Mercy and Courage are fixed. The Japanese confirmation screen shows Shi Jin with Body 100, Strength 93, Dexterity 83, Wisdom 49, Integrity 74, Mercy 69 and Courage 95.OBSERVED

Characters VERIFIED

There are 255 characters, so the novel's 108 heroes are only part of the cast. Every character uses the same attributes:6

  • Body 体力, Strength 腕力, Dexterity 技量, Wisdom 知力: abilities, max 100. Body works like hit points and is spent by some commands.
  • Integrity 忠義, Mercy 仁愛, Courage 勇気: fixed personality values, max 100.
  • Popularity: max 999. It drives the Imperial Edict.
  • Loyalty: how firmly a follower is bound to you.
  • Also Men (troops led), Steersman (river skill), Ship, Position and Serves (whom they follow).

Characters also carry a social label. The screenshots show "young master" (若旦那), "elder" (長者), "outlaw" (無法者) and, on the NES, "Robed Scholar".OBSERVED The reverse-engineered Mac save data stores each hero as a 24-byte record and marks them as ruler, recruit or townsperson.13

"Skill" is the prefecture's troop training. The personal fighting stat is Dexterity.

Turns: one command per prefecture per month VERIFIED

The game runs in monthly turns, starting in January. Each occupied prefecture gets one command per month, and the order changes randomly every month. The NES calls each slot a "rotation". You give orders directly in your home prefecture and in any prefecture held by a sworn Brother or Sister. Other chieftains act on their own under a standing order: Entrust, Develop or Expand.57

The Japanese prompt reads "your orders (0–16)?".OBSERVED A Good Fellow in exile, with no prefecture, gets a much shorter menu: the DOS screen shows Li Kui in exile with "your orders (0–6)?".OBSERVED

Economy, seasons and events VERIFIED

  • Taxes in gold and food come once a year, in January, and only if Support is at least 40. The NES manual contradicts itself on whether exactly 40 counts.
  • Seasons change in March, June, September and December. Spring is March–May, summer June–August, autumn September–November, winter December–February.
  • Seasonal events:
    • summer typhoons
    • winter snowstorms, which block Move and Fight
    • festivals in spring and autumn
    • wild beasts
    • epidemics, in any season
  • Riots can break out any month while Support is between 40 and 55, and troops may desert.
  • Gao Qiu's demands. The minister periodically demands about a quarter to a third of a prefecture's gold.7 SINGLE SOURCE

Recruiting and loyalty SINGLE SOURCE

  • Downtown has three options:
    • Recruit someone in town
    • Divine whether a person is willing to join (1 gold each)
    • buy Rumors about 7 people elsewhere (10 gold)
    The Japanese screenshot shows exactly these three: 仲間にする / 可能性を占う / 噂話を聞く.OBSERVED
  • Newcomers who arrive at the start of a season may ask to join you.
  • Gifts of gold and Feasts raise loyalty; above 50 gold per gift the effect drops off.
  • A hero with loyalty 95+ can become a sworn Brother. Brothers' prefectures take your direct orders.
  • Invite merges another Good Fellow into your band, for 150–250 gold per hero.
  • Captured enemies can be recruited, imprisoned, exiled or executed.10

Popularity SINGLE SOURCE

Each of your prefectures with Support of 40 or more adds 12 Popularity, and you lose those 12 if its Support drops below 40. Killing a beast adds 5 (3 for a failed attempt). Taking Liangshan (Lian Shan Bo, the outlaws' marsh fortress in the novel) adds 100. A failed recruitment costs 1. Gao Qiu's home is usually prefecture 23.7

Battles on a hex map VERIFIED

A battle lasts one month, split into 30 daily turns, and each hero gets one command per day. Each hex holds one unit (a hero and their men), and each side fields at most 10 units. The attacker places units on 10 deployment hexes; the defender chooses among 20 numbered hexes.5

  • Fireball needs Wisdom 60+ and doesn't work in rain or snow. Magic needs Wisdom 80+, Body 50+ and a cloudy day. A Duel is one-on-one until one hero's Body reaches 0.

    • capturing or driving off the defending ruler
    • holding all castles for a full turn
    • starving the defenders
  • The attacker loses early if it runs out of food or its own ruler is captured or flees.

Contemporary reviewers found the game very close to KOEI's Romance of the Three Kingdoms; one called it "too similar".9 The bandit-hero theme, the Edict and the 1127 deadline are what set it apart.

04Art, colour and sound

Portraits SINGLE SOURCE

The portrait files hold 260 portraits on the PC-98, X68000 and Chinese DOS versions (255 characters plus extras) and 258 on the PC-88, which lacks the two emperors. PC-98 portraits are 64×80 pixels; PC-88 and Chinese DOS store them at half height, 64×40. All use 3 bits per pixel, which means 8 colours.15

The style is dramatic Japanese illustration of the late 1980s:OBSERVED

  • strong black outlines and exaggerated, expressive faces, such as Lu Zhishen as a scowling bald monk
  • shading built from dither patterns
  • each portrait mounted like a hanging scroll, in a parchment frame with gold rollers

Palette and texture OBSERVED

The Japanese PC screens look like a classic 8-colour digital palette: black, blue, red, magenta, green, cyan, yellow and white. Every in-between tone (pale green land, tan, orange, brown mountains) is an ordered or stippled dither of two of those colours. Faction colours on the map are flat fills. Text is white, with yellow labels, on solid black windows.

Ornament carries a lot of the period feel:

  • red-and-gold woven or zigzag borders around the screen
  • green bamboo poles around the message line
  • green theatre curtains around the picture window
  • a red Greek-key band on the NES title screen

Battle tiles are textured hex icons with a seasonal palette: in winter, plains turn white and lakes ice-cyan.

Illustrations and box art OBSERVED

  • Event vignettes. Small square pictures (a tavern, cheering soldiers, a boar hunt). Reviewers called them "humorous animations".8
  • Box and store art. Painted in a dramatic Western fantasy-poster style, with a looming bearded figure over a crowd of heroes and dragons.
  • 1996 remake. Swaps all of this for anime cel animation and painted textures.

05Remake evaluation

Rules documentation
Strong. Two official manuals, a detailed strategy guide and reverse-engineered data layouts.
Technical difficulty
Low to moderate. A 49-node graph, a few hundred records, a monthly loop and a small hex battle. Nothing needs a game engine.
Computer opponents and balance
Moderate to high. Rival chieftains, Gao Qiu's behaviour and diplomacy need tuning. This is where KOEI's games got their feel.
Art and audio
High. Portraits are the biggest single job. You can cut the cast well below 255.
Legal risk
Low for an original game in the same genre and setting. High if you reuse KOEI's name, art, music, text or stat tables, or copy its screens closely.INFERENCE

What you can use and what you must make

Free to use

  • The Water Margin story, its 108 heroes, Gao Qiu, Liangshan Marsh and Song-dynasty geography. The novel dates from the 14th century, with a complete 1589 printed edition, far beyond any copyright term.21 Use your own wording and romanisation, not a modern translation's.
  • General mechanics: monthly turns, prefecture economies, recruiting, loyalty, a deadline, hex battles with weather and fire. Copyright protects a game's artistic expression, not its rules.1617
  • Real history: the Jurchen invasion of 1127, Emperor Huizong and Kaifeng.

Make it yourself

  • The name. Don't use "Bandit Kings of Ancient China" or "Suikoden"; Konami holds and renewed the "Suikoden" trademark in 2022.20
  • All art: portraits, map, tiles, vignettes, title and box art.
  • All music and sound, including new melodies, not arrangements of KOEI's.
  • All text: event messages and descriptions.
  • Screen layout and look. Don't reproduce KOEI's framed panels pixel for pixel; design your own.
  • Numbers. Set the stats for each character and the starting scenario values yourself, for example derived from the novel, not copied from the game data.

Legal notes MIXED SOURCES

This is research, not legal advice. Get a lawyer's view before you publish anything commercially.

  • Close look-and-feel copies can infringe. In Tetris Holding v. Xio Interactive (US, 2012), the court held that copyright covers a game's artistic aspects, not its mechanics. It still found a clone infringing because its visual expression was "akin to literal copying", even though no code or images were copied. It also upheld a trade-dress claim.16 Being "inspired by" is fine; copying the screens closely is risky.
  • KOEI Tecmo enforces its copyright. It sued the parent company of Chinese publisher Bekko Games over KOEI screenshots used in Bekko's ads. Bekko settled, apologised, deleted the images and paid ¥5 million.18 KOEI's legal department has also said publicly that it tolerates fan works in general but removes thousands of inappropriate ones each year.19 I found no record of KOEI acting against a fan remake of an old title, but that only means none was found.
  • The game is still on sale. KOEI Tecmo sells it on Steam, so it is an active commercial product, not abandonware.1
  • Data in the EU. If you publish from the EU, the sui generis database right can protect a substantial extraction of a database that took real investment. Copying the full 255-character stat table and scenario data could fall under it. Designing your own values avoids the question.INFERENCE
  • Permission. If you want the original name or art, the only safe route is to ask KOEI Tecmo.17

Existing projects VERIFIED

No playable fan remake, open-source clone or browser version turned up. There are three hobbyist reverse-engineering projects. They are useful for understanding the rules and data shapes, but not for copying data or assets:

  • danhealy/bandit_kings_modding: Ruby scripts that read and write the Mac version's 21,122-byte save and scenario files. They document 24-byte hero records, 32-byte prefecture records and 28-byte tactical records.13
  • chrisaacson69/bk-decompiler: a decompilation of the NES version. Its claim to be complete could not be confirmed.14
  • tzengyuxio/kaodata: an MIT-licensed tool that extracts portraits and character data from your own copy of KOEI games. The tool's licence does not cover the extracted art, which still belongs to KOEI Tecmo.15

Recommended web stack INFERENCE

  • TypeScript + Vite, deployed as a static site (GitHub Pages, Netlify or Cloudflare Pages). Single-player and hot-seat need no server, just like the original.
  • A pure, deterministic rules engine with no DOM code. Game state is plain JSON, and a seeded random generator drives events and combat. You get save games, replays and unit tests (Vitest) almost for free.
  • The strategic map in SVG. 49 prefecture shapes are easy to click, recolour by faction and scale on any screen.
  • The battle in Canvas 2D (or PixiJS if you want lots of animation). Use axial hex coordinates; Red Blob Games' hexagon guide covers movement cost, line of sight and pathfinding. With at most 20 units on a small map, performance is not an issue.
  • Menus and panels in plain HTML/CSS, or a light framework such as Svelte or Preact. Keep number-key shortcuts as a nod to the original.
  • Saves in IndexedDB, with JSON export so players can back them up.
  • Computer turns in a Web Worker if the AI gets heavy, so the page stays responsive.
  • Audio through the Web Audio API. Use OGG files, or tracker modules with a JS player if you want a chiptune or FM feel.

A sensible build order INFERENCE

  1. Data model and rules engine: prefectures, characters, monthly loop, January taxes, seasons and decay. Test it headless.
  2. The SVG strategic map and the three-panel main screen, with the 16 commands.
  3. The hex battle: deploy, move, attack, weather, fire, and the 30-day limit.
  4. Computer chieftains, Gao Qiu's behaviour, diplomacy and recruiting.
  5. Popularity, the Imperial Edict, the 1127 deadline and the endings.
  6. Art and audio, replacing placeholders.

06Open questions and caveats

  • Version differences in the rules. Battle numbers and resource caps come from the NES manual only. The PC, Amiga and 1996 versions may differ.
  • Composer. Not confirmed; the album page gives no credits.
  • Display specs for the Mac, Amiga and MSX2 versions are not confirmed.
  • Meaning of some values. The exact meaning of "People" and of the second and third rows on the battle force display is unconfirmed.
  • Trademarks. Whether KOEI Tecmo still holds a trademark on "Bandit Kings of Ancient China" was not checked.
  • Release dates. The original is dated 1988 or 1989 depending on the source, and the MSX2 date is August or November 1989.
  • How this was researched. A multi-agent search fetched 19 sources and extracted 83 claims. 25 claims were put through a three-vote check: 20 were confirmed and 5 refuted. The screenshot and art findings come from my own look at 14 screenshots. MobyGames and Hall of Light blocked automated access, so their galleries are linked but were not checked.
